# Question bank

Kinds of question that reviewers, officials, academics and practitioners raise when a curriculum, framework or policy paper is put in front of them. Gathered from earlier projects and rewritten in general form: no names, no organisations, no document titles, no wording copied from anyone.

**How to use it.** Read it before writing prep pages. For each entry, write this document's version of that question, naming the real section, item or table. Skip entries that do not apply. The project's own past-question material comes first; this bank fills what that material does not cover. Never paste an entry into a wiki as it stands.

**Adding to it.** Only when the user asks. Add the general form of a question, under the fitting heading, with nothing that identifies the project it came from.

"The document" below means whatever is being defended. "Item" means its smallest numbered unit: a competency, a standard, an article, a measure.

## Name, status and force

- What is the correct title? Several versions are in circulation.
- Is this a curriculum, a new subject, a guideline or a binding rule?
- Is it mandatory? From what date? For which bodies, including private ones and those under other authorities?
- What is its legal status, and what happens to a unit that does nothing?
- If the government or the leadership changes, does it survive?

## Scope and coverage

- Does it cover this other group too (older or younger learners, non-formal settings, staff as well as learners)? Why does it stop where it stops?
- The field has moved far ahead at the advanced level. Is this enough for that level?
- The word for the target group is broad. Should the scope be widened to match it, or the word narrowed?
- Will there be a later phase for the groups left out? Where is that written?

## Out of date

- Technology and practice in this field change fast. Will this be obsolete in three years?
- The newest development in the field is not mentioned. Is the document already behind?
- This item describes how today's tools happen to work. Is it a lasting ability or a passing technique?
- If the technology becomes the basis of everything, is an item that asks "should it be used at all?" still needed?
- Is there a review cycle? Who triggers a revision?

## Overlap and redundancy

- Several items look alike. Which ones overlap, and can they be assessed separately?
- Does this duplicate an existing subject, an existing literacy or competency scheme, or a course already on offer?
- Does it duplicate a national plan or the work of another ministry or agency?
- Are there too many items for anyone to remember and use? Why this many?
- Why this number of top-level parts? What would be lost with one fewer?

## Structure and naming

- Why is this cross-cutting theme (ethics, safety, equity) not a part of its own?
- Should a new part be added, or an existing part's name be lengthened, to cover governance, safety and security?
- Why was a fashionable term avoided, and what replaces it?
- A key outcome named in a higher-level national standard (for example innovation) is hardly visible here. Why?
- Where does this particular idea sit? Which item covers it?

## Wording of items and levels

- The verbs at each level ("explain", "discuss", "be aware of") are not observable or measurable, and do not follow a recognised taxonomy. How were they chosen?
- Are the levels a real developmental progression, or a ladder taken from somewhere else or invented?
- Is the level description detailed enough to build a scoring rubric from?
- Proposal to add a qualifying clause to one level of one item: a limitation, a resource condition, a local-context condition.
- Proposal to reverse the order of steps in an item, because the present order anchors the learner's thinking.
- Proposal to add one concrete check to an item, such as confirming that something cited exists or identifying where a piece of feedback came from.

## Expectations by group or stage

- Where do the minimum levels for each stage come from: evidence of what learners can do, or expert judgement?
- Can the youngest group meaningfully do this?
- Learners have little background knowledge. How can they be expected to evaluate or judge?
- Should the academic track and the vocational track be held to the same level on these items?
- Why is this item already at a higher level than its neighbours at the first stage, and why does it then not move?
- Why do these items stop below the top level at the final stage?
- Why is this whole part capped below the top level?
- An item on a public-good theme (sustainability, wellbeing) is set low for most stages. Is it an afterthought?

## Origins and local fit

- Is this a translation of a foreign framework?
- Is following foreign models again right for this country? Whose assumptions come with them?
- What here is truly local, and is that label deserved? Are a few local items out of many enough?
- Is the table mapping items to international frameworks a real comparison, or labels attached afterwards?
- "It covers every international standard": how is that shown?
- One of the source frameworks is brand new and untested. Why lean on it?
- A benchmark used for one stage is still a draft. What if its final form differs?
- Which national plan or standard authorises this, and does the rationale section cite it?
- Who reviewed it before today, and where did their comments go?
- The references contain errors. Were they checked?

## The core concept

- Is the central idea a real competency or a fashionable word turned into a framework?
- Are the local-values items competencies, or values dressed as competencies? Can they be assessed?
- Why should the state define how young people relate to products made by private companies?
- Will this make learners dependent, less able to think for themselves, or more exposed to screens?

## Who does the work

- Who can teach this? It is hard, and staff have not been trained.
- How much work does it add, in practice?
- Does it need a new subject or more hours?
- Which staff are responsible for which items?
- Are there too many items for one person to develop in learners?
- What must a practitioner do differently next Monday?
- Does it collide with duties and reporting that units already carry?
- What does it ask of leaders, supervisors, parents, and the institutions that train staff?

## Equity and readiness

- Small, remote, under-resourced units have no equipment and no connection. How do they comply?
- Will this widen the gap between well-resourced and poorly resourced units?
- If adoption is tiered, does the lowest tier quietly accept that poor units stay there for good?
- How is an item that needs equipment assessed where there is none?

## Money, ownership and follow-through

- What does it cost, whose budget is it in, and is any money committed?
- Who owns it after the drafting body hands it over, and who revises it?
- Who is accountable if it fails?
- What support reaches units, and when? What if the promised support does not come?
- How is this different from policies that were announced and died within two years?

## Assessment and evidence

- How are these assessed in practice, especially attitudes such as integrity or moderation?
- How is a learner stopped from letting a tool do the whole task?
- How will the country know it worked?
- Will results be used to rank or punish staff and units? What prevents that?
- Who builds the central assessment tools, and by when?
- Does tying it to an international test mean teaching to the test? Will it raise the country's scores, and when?

## Safety and data

- How is personal data protected when units have learners use external tools?
- What does the document say to families worried about risk and overuse?

## Phrasing to expect

Questions in the room rarely arrive in the form above. Expect:

- **A few blunt words**: "Is it compulsory?", "Who is going to teach this?", "Copying foreigners again."
- **A topic with no question**: "Inequality. Poor schools." The user needs the page, fast.
- **A proposal presented as a question**: "Shouldn't this include…?"
- **A numbered list of observations** covering title, scope, a missing word and a missing citation in one turn.
- **A drafting suggestion** aimed at one level of one item, with replacement wording offered.
- **A challenge to method** from a specialist in assessment or curriculum design.

Prepared answers and the `/ask` helper must work for all of these, including the two-word ones.
